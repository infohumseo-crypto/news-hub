Keyword Research for Beginners: A Free Step-by-Step Guide

Keyword research is the process of finding the words and phrases people type into search engines, so you can write articles that answer those searches. Skip it and you may write great posts that nobody ever looks for. Do it well and every article has a clear audience before you write a single sentence.

What is a keyword?

A keyword is simply the topic or phrase a person searches for, such as "how to start a blog" or "best free photo editor". Some are short (one or two words) and very competitive. Others are long and specific, called long-tail keywords, and they are usually the best place for a new blog to start.

Step 1: List your seed topics

Write down 5 to 10 broad topics your blog covers. If your blog is about personal finance, your seeds might be "saving money", "budgeting" and "student loans". These seeds are only starting points that you will narrow later.

Step 2: Expand them with free tools

You do not need paid software to begin. These free sources show real searches:

  • Google autocomplete: type a seed topic and note the suggestions that appear.
  • People also ask and related searches: found on the results page, they reveal the questions readers have.
  • Google Search Console: once your site has traffic, it shows the queries you already appear for.
  • Google Trends: compares topics over time and shows whether interest is growing.
  • Google Keyword Planner: free to use with a Google Ads account and useful for rough search volume ranges.

Step 3: Understand search intent

Search intent is the reason behind a search. Someone typing "what is SEO" wants an explanation. Someone typing "best SEO course" is comparing options. Before choosing a keyword, search it yourself and look at what ranks. If the top results are step-by-step guides, write a step-by-step guide. If they are product lists, a long essay will not match what people want.

Step 4: Check how hard it is to rank

Look at the first page of results. If it is full of large, well-known websites, a new blog will struggle. If you see forum threads, small blogs or thin pages, there is probably room for a better article. Prefer specific phrases like "keyword research for a new blog" over broad ones like "SEO".

Step 5: Pick one main keyword per page

Give each article one main keyword and a few closely related phrases. Place the main keyword in the title, the first paragraph, one or two subheadings and the URL. Write naturally. Repeating a phrase again and again makes the text worse for readers and does not help rankings.

Step 6: Build a simple content plan

Put your chosen keywords in a spreadsheet with columns for the keyword, the intent, the planned title and the publish date. Aim to cover one topic from several angles, and link those articles to each other. For ideas on how to organize and publish on a schedule, read How to Start a Blog That People Actually Read.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Targeting only high-volume keywords that big sites already dominate.
  • Ignoring intent and writing the wrong type of page.
  • Using several articles to target the same keyword, which makes them compete with each other.
  • Chasing numbers instead of reader value.

Frequently asked questions

How many keywords should one post target? One main keyword and a handful of related phrases that fit naturally.

Do I need paid tools? No. Free tools are enough to start. Paid tools mainly save time and add estimates.

How long until I see results? New sites often need several months. Keep publishing and improving your posts.

Conclusion

Good keyword research is just good listening. Find the questions your readers ask, check what they expect to see, and answer better than the pages already ranking. Next, make sure each post is ready to rank with our on-page SEO checklist.
