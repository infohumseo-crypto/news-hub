How to Write SEO Titles and Meta Descriptions That Get Clicks

Your title and meta description are your advertisement in search results. People decide in a second whether to click, so these two short pieces of text matter a lot. This guide shows simple ways to write them well.

Title tag vs. headline

The title tag is the text shown in search results and the browser tab. The headline (H1) is the title displayed on the page. They are often the same, but they can differ slightly. Both should clearly describe the page.

Title tag best practices

  • Put the main keyword near the beginning. It tells searchers and search engines what the page is about.
  • Keep it concise. Titles of roughly 50 to 60 characters usually display fully, although the real limit depends on pixel width.
  • Be specific. "Keyword Research for Beginners: A Free Step-by-Step Guide" beats "Keyword Tips".
  • Make a promise you keep. Misleading titles raise bounce rates and hurt trust.
  • Make every title unique. Duplicate titles confuse readers and search engines.

Five simple title formulas

  1. How to + result: How to Start a Blog That People Actually Read
  2. Number + benefit: 15 Things to Check Before You Publish
  3. Guide + audience: SEO Guide for Small Business Owners
  4. Question: What Is Search Intent and Why Does It Matter?
  5. Problem + solution: Slow Website? 7 Quick Fixes

Meta description best practices

A meta description is a short summary of the page. Search engines often show it under your title, although they may replace it with text from the page if they think it fits the query better. A good one:

  • Is around 120 to 160 characters long.
  • Includes the main keyword naturally, since matching words may appear in bold.
  • States a clear benefit, such as what the reader will learn or be able to do.
  • Ends with a gentle invitation, like "Learn how" or "See the full checklist".
  • Is unique for every page.

Before and after examples

Weak: "Blog post about SEO and some tips for you."

Better: "Learn the on-page SEO basics: titles, headings, links and images. Use this 15-step checklist before every post goes live."

A note on meta keywords

The old meta keywords tag is ignored by major search engines, so you do not need it. Put your effort into the title, description and page content instead.

How to test and improve

In Google Search Console, open the Performance report and look at pages with many impressions but a low click-through rate. Those are good candidates for a new title or description. Change one thing at a time and give it a few weeks before judging the result.

Conclusion

Write for humans first: be clear, specific and honest. A strong title and description will not rescue weak content, but they help the right readers find your good content. To review the rest of your page, use our on-page SEO checklist.
