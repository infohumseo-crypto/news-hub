How to Get Backlinks to a New Website Without Spending Money

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Search engines treat quality links as a sign that other people find your content useful. For a new blog, earning a few good links can make a big difference, and you do not have to pay for them.

Quality beats quantity

One link from a relevant, trusted site is worth more than hundreds from spammy ones. Avoid buying links or joining link exchange schemes, since search engines may treat these as manipulation and they can hurt your site.

1. Create content worth linking to

People link to things that are useful, clear and unique. Original research, free templates, checklists, calculators and in-depth guides tend to attract links naturally. Start by improving what you already have, for example by turning the advice in our on-page SEO checklist into a printable resource.

2. Write guest posts for relevant sites

Find blogs in your niche that accept contributions. Read their guidelines, pitch a specific idea and offer a truly useful article. Most sites allow one link back to your site in the author bio or where it fits naturally.

3. Be a source for journalists and bloggers

Writers often look for expert quotes. If you have real experience, share it through public requests, social media or by replying to relevant questions. A helpful comment can earn a mention and a link.

4. Use broken link building

Find a relevant page that links to a dead resource. Politely tell the site owner and suggest your article as a replacement if it truly fits. This helps them fix a problem, so it often gets a better response than a plain request.

5. List your site in trusted places

Add your site to legitimate profiles and directories related to your topic, such as your social profiles, community pages and industry listings. Choose real, relevant places and avoid low-quality link farms.

6. Build relationships

Comment thoughtfully on other blogs, share their work and join communities in your niche. Real relationships lead to mentions over time. Be patient and genuine rather than asking for links straight away.

7. Promote your best content

Great articles do not help if nobody sees them. Share them on social media, in newsletters and in relevant communities where self-promotion is allowed. More visibility means more chances to be linked.

How to write an outreach email

  • Use the person's name and mention something specific about their site.
  • Explain clearly why your page is useful to their readers.
  • Keep it short and polite, with no pressure.
  • Follow up once, then move on.

How to track your links

Google Search Console has a Links report that shows which sites link to you, at no cost. Check it monthly to see what content attracts links and write more like it.

Frequently asked questions

How many backlinks do I need? There is no fixed number. It depends on how competitive your topic is. Focus on earning relevant links steadily.

Are nofollow links useless? No. They can still bring traffic and awareness, even if they pass less ranking signal.

Conclusion

Link building is a long-term habit, not a quick trick. Publish helpful content, be generous in your community and ask politely when it makes sense. Make sure your site is ready first by reading our SEO basics guide.
